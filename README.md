# irk_bitig
Irk Bitig (𐰃𐰺𐰴 𐰋𐰃𐱅𐰃𐰏‎) Book of Omens. Translations and linguistic analysis. 

From [Wikisource](https://wikisource.org/wiki/%F0%90%B0%83%F0%90%B0%BA%F0%90%B0%B4_%F0%90%B0%8B%F0%90%B0%83%F0%90%B1%85%F0%90%B0%83%F0%90%B0%8F):

Irk Bitig, known as the "Book of Omens" or "Book of Divination" in English, is a 9th-century manuscript book on divination that was discovered in the Library Cave of the Mogao Caves in Dunhuang, China, by Aurel Stein in 1907, and is now in the collection of the British Library. The text comprises sixty-five omens, each represented by a group of three sequences of between one and four circles, followed by the divination text, terminated by a prognostication (missing in a few cases). After the final divination is a concluding sentence; and at the very end is a colophon which states that the text was written on the 15th day of the 2nd month of the year of the tiger at the Taygüntan Manichaean monastery by an anonymous monk for his "elder brother", General İtaçuk (Saŋun İtaçuk).
